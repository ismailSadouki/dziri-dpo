Generate 20 candidate instruction-response examples for Algerian Darija in the category `reasoning`.

The goal is to create high-quality supervised fine-tuning data for an Algerian Darija language model.

## Core language requirements

1. The user message must be naturally phrased in Algerian Darija.
2. The assistant response must be naturally phrased in Algerian Darija.
3. The dataset MUST contain realistic variation in writing style and script.
4. Do NOT generate all examples in Arabic script.
5. Use this target script distribution:
   - 12 primarily Arabic script
   - 4 primarily Arabizi/Latin script
   - 4 mixed Arabic + natural French/English code-switching
6. Arabizi examples must look like realistic Algerian online writing, not mechanical transliteration.
7. Arabic-script examples must sound authentically Algerian, not Modern Standard Arabic.
8. Mixed examples may naturally contain French or English technical/common terms when Algerians would realistically use them.
9. Do not force French or English into every mixed example.
10. Do not use Moroccan or Tunisian Darija.
11. Avoid excessive MSA vocabulary and sentence structure.

## Reasoning category

Focus on problems where the user expects the assistant to think through the situation rather than simply retrieve a fact.

Include a diverse mixture of:

- everyday logical reasoning
- cause-and-effect reasoning
- comparing two or more options
- practical decision-making
- prioritization
- planning
- identifying contradictions
- interpreting situations
- common-sense reasoning
- simple estimation
- basic quantitative reasoning
- multi-step reasoning
- explaining why one option makes more sense than another
- reasoning about consequences
- choosing between alternatives under constraints

Examples should feel like questions a real Algerian person could ask an assistant.

Good examples include situations such as:
- deciding which task to do first when time is limited
- figuring out why a plan failed
- comparing two practical options
- reasoning from several pieces of information
- identifying what conclusion follows from a situation
- estimating quantities or time
- deciding what information is missing before making a decision
- spotting an inconsistency in someone's explanation

Do NOT make the dataset primarily mathematical.
Do NOT turn every example into a puzzle.
Do NOT use artificial IQ-test questions.
Do NOT make every question explicitly say "explain your reasoning".
The reasoning should arise naturally from the user's question.

## Difficulty

Use mostly easy-to-medium reasoning.

Some examples should require multiple reasoning steps, but the answer should remain understandable and natural.

Do not generate advanced university mathematics, formal proofs, programming problems, or highly specialized technical reasoning.

## Response quality

1. The assistant must actually solve the reasoning problem.
2. Give enough explanation to make the conclusion understandable.
3. Do not provide unexplained conclusions.
4. Keep responses reasonably concise.
5. Avoid generic filler.
6. Do not invent facts that are unnecessary for the reasoning.
7. If the problem does not contain enough information for a definite conclusion, say so naturally and explain what information is missing.
8. When multiple answers are possible, acknowledge the relevant uncertainty.
9. Use natural Algerian conversational phrasing.
10. Vary response structure and length.
11. Do not repeat the same reasoning pattern across examples.
12. Avoid artificial benchmark-style wording.
