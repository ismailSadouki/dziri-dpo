Generate 20 candidate instruction-response examples for Algerian Darija in the category `translation`.

The goal is to create high-quality supervised fine-tuning data for an Algerian Darija language model.

## Core language requirements

1. The user message must be naturally phrased in Algerian Darija OR naturally ask for a translation involving Algerian Darija.
2. The assistant response must be naturally written and must provide the requested translation.
3. The dataset MUST contain realistic variation in writing style and script.
4. Do NOT generate all examples in Arabic script.
5. Use this target script distribution:

   * 12 primarily Arabic script
   * 4 primarily Arabizi/Latin script
   * 4 mixed Arabic + natural French/English code-switching
6. Arabizi examples must look like realistic Algerian online writing, not mechanical transliteration.
7. Algerian Darija output must sound authentically Algerian, not Moroccan, Tunisian, or MSA.
8. Do not force French or English into examples where it would sound unnatural.

## Translation directions

Include a diverse mixture of translation directions involving Algerian Darija.

Use combinations such as:

* English → Algerian Darija
* French → Algerian Darija
* MSA/Arabic → Algerian Darija
* Algerian Darija → English
* Algerian Darija → French
* Algerian Darija → MSA/Arabic

Most examples should involve Algerian Darija directly.

Do NOT make all 20 examples English → Darija.

Target a varied distribution of translation directions.

## Translation quality

The assistant should translate for MEANING and naturalness, not simply replace words one by one.

1. Preserve the meaning of the source.
2. Preserve important details.
3. Preserve the intended tone and register.
4. Use natural Algerian vocabulary and sentence structure.
5. Do not unnecessarily translate proper names.
6. Do not add information that is absent from the source.
7. Do not remove important information.
8. Preserve questions as questions and requests as requests.
9. Preserve politeness level when relevant.
10. When the source is informal, the translation should normally remain informal.
11. When the source is formal, use an appropriately formal target register.
12. If there are multiple natural translations, choose a natural Algerian formulation rather than a literal one.

## Algerian Darija specificity

The Algerian Darija translation should sound like something an Algerian person would actually say.

Avoid:

* Moroccan vocabulary
* Tunisian vocabulary
* overly literal MSA structures
* unnatural dictionary translations
* textbook-style "translated Darija"
* unnecessarily formal language

Use authentic Algerian expressions where appropriate.

Examples may naturally contain:

* French-derived vocabulary
* common Algerian code-switching
* regional or conversational expressions

But do not insert French words merely to make the translation look Algerian.

## Code-switching

Some source sentences should naturally lead to Algerian Darija translations containing French or English terms.

Examples may involve concepts such as:

* rendez-vous
* téléphone
* ordinateur
* application
* travail
* weekend
* deadline
* internet
* problème
* message

Only use code-switching when it sounds natural.

Do NOT artificially mix languages in every translation.

## Register diversity

Include a mixture of:

* casual conversation
* polite requests
* messages to friends
* workplace communication
* simple explanations
* questions
* short announcements
* practical everyday sentences
* moderately formal communication

Do not make every example a casual sentence.

## Difficulty and length

Use mostly short-to-medium source texts.

Include:

* simple sentences
* two-sentence messages
* short conversational exchanges
* a few slightly longer messages

Do NOT generate long paragraphs.

The goal is natural translation behavior, not document translation.

## Important translation behavior

Some examples should test:

* idiomatic expressions
* politeness
* tense
* negation
* questions
* requests
* emotional tone
* informal language
* ambiguous expressions where context matters
* French/English words commonly used in Algerian speech

For idioms:

Do NOT translate the idiom literally if that would sound unnatural in Algerian Darija.

Instead, provide the closest natural Algerian expression that preserves the meaning.

## Handling ambiguity

If a source sentence genuinely has more than one reasonable interpretation:

1. Choose the most natural interpretation from the context.
2. If the ambiguity materially changes the translation, briefly mention the alternative.
3. Do not invent context that was not provided.

Do not turn every example into an explanation about ambiguity.

## Response format

The assistant should normally provide the translation directly.

For example:

User:
"Translate this to Algerian Darija: I’ll call you when I get home."

Assistant:
"نعيطلك كي نوصل للدار."

Do not unnecessarily provide:

* word-by-word analysis
* grammar lessons
* long explanations
* multiple alternatives

unless the example specifically tests ambiguity or register.
